This is the wrong location for an AI data centre.

A 2.4 gw hyperscale ai data centre has been proposed on agricultural land beside Travers Reservoir in the county of Vulcan. This is an Environmentally Significant Area, a critical irrigation resource, home to one of Alberta’s most respected children’s summer camp and the nearby community of Little Bow.

We’re the families that have lived in the area for more than 30 years and we're asking decision-makers to choose a different Site. Once this location is opened to industrial development there will be no going back.

Know the facts

The Proposal

Here's what has been filed, what it would mean for the land around Travers Reservoir/Little Bow, and where the process stands today. We update this page as new filings become public always with a source.

What's being proposed

The proposal is to build a 2.4 GW AI data centre campus on agricultural land adjoining Travers Reservoir. A project of this kind requires a substantial, continuous electrical power supply and significant water for cooling, along with new supporting infrastructure.

The proponent's application states the project would bring investment and jobs to the region. Community members have asked for clearer evidence on water sourcing and allocation, power infrastructure requirements, the amount of productive farmland that would be taken out of production, and the long-term economic case We are tracking these concerns, more in the depth questions are raised on the Issues page.

Community concerns raised so far

  • Loss of some of Alberta's most productive farmland to heavy industrial development.
  • Water demand for cooling competing with an irrigation allocation that is already fully committed.
  • New high-capacity power infrastructure and generation needed to serve the facility.
  • Emergency egress with single road access for the community.
  • Noise, light, and 24/7 operations next to a rural residential and recreational area.
  • Limited detail so far on independent water and environmental impact assessments.
  • Questions about long-term local economic benefit relative to the footprint and resources required.

The Issues

The questions we're asking, organized by topic. Each section links to the research, filings, and organizations behind it. We'll keep adding sources as the record grows.

Environmental+

Beyond water use, a facility of this scale raises broader ecological questions for the land and reservoir it would sit beside. We're asking what independent environmental assessment has been done on Travers Reservoir and the surrounding watershed, how the development would affect wildlife habitat and bird corridors in the area, and what safeguards are proposed for air quality, noise, and light pollution from a facility that would run continuously, day and night.

  • Alberta Utilities Commission — project filings
  • Independent environmental assessment status
Water+

Cooling a facility of this scale requires a large, continuous water supply. We're asking what source is proposed, how it interacts with irrigation allocations that are already fully committed in this district, and what independent assessment exists of the impact on Travers Reservoir, the surrounding watershed, and downstream users.

  • South Saskatchewan River irrigation district
  • Local irrigation district — water allocation background
Agricultural Land+

Agricultural production in Vulcan County is a top economic driver in Alberta, anchored by grain crops, oilseeds, and livestock operations. The local economy relies heavily on extensive dryland and irrigated farming, supported by regional water resources like the Bow River Irrigation District (BRID).

A vital component of the BRID system is the Travers Reservoir which was created in the 1950's by building the Travers Dam on the Little Bow River.

Water is initially diverted from the Bow River at the Carseland Weir, moves via the main canal into Lake McGregor, through a connecting canal into Travers Reservoir and the Little Bow Reservoir. This system provides irrigation to over 290,000 acres of farmland.

The County of Vulcan includes over 1.01 million improved acres operated by approximately 1,050 farms. Vulcan County ranks as the third-highest overall gross agricultural-producing municipality in Alberta, historically reporting hundreds of millions in gross farm receipts, according to Statistics Canada data.

  • Provincial farmland loss data
Power & Energy+

A facility like this requires a large, continuous electrical supply. We're asking what new generation or transmission infrastructure would be needed, who pays for it, and how it fits within the province's broader grid planning.

  • Alberta Electric System Operator planning documents
Legal & Regulatory+

We're tracking the approvals this project needs provincial, municipal, and otherwise and where each one stands. This includes county land-use bylaws, utility and environmental approvals, and any opportunities for public and landowner input at each stage.

  • Our filings & submissions
  • County of Vulcan
Safety+

Residents have also raised serious public safety concerns, including the potential for fire, and the fact that the only access and evacuation route passes directly into and beside the proposed industrial site.  Other concerns with this industrial development include noise, lighting, air, and water pollution.

  • County emergency services planning
Our story

History of Little Bow

Over 70 years ago

Travers Reservoir established

Travers reservoir was established over 70 years ago and has become a critical part of the irrigation system in southern Alberta, supplying water to 300,000 acres of farm land.

Following the reservoir

Little Bow parks established

Once the reservoir was complete, governments then established Little Bow Provincial Park and Little Bow Provincial Recreation Area to be used for recreational purposes. These parks are visited by thousands of Albertans every year.

1980s

Environmentally Significant Area designation

Given the unique nature of the man-made lake and these parks, the government recognized the area surrounding Travers reservoir as an Environmentally Significant Area (ESA) in the 1980s. The ESA now provides habitat for several species at risk.

1981

Southern Alberta Bible Camp founded

The Southern Alberta Bible Camp (SABC) was established on the north arm of the lake in 1981 and has become one Alberta's most respected childrens' summer camps.

1990

Little Bow community founded

In 1990, the first houses were built in the nearby community of Little Bow, which now includes 250 homes.

June 2026

Farmland rezoned for industrial use

In June of 2026, Vulcan County Council rezoned approximately 595 acres of farm land to industrial use, making way for one of the largest AI Data Centre developments contemplated in the province. The proposed development, a 2.4 GW AI Data Centre, would be located adjacent to the Travers Reservoir in Vulcan County.
